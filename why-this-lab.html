WHY THIS LAB

This is not just an AI problem. It is a delivery problem.

Research from RAND, McKinsey, PMI, and DORA points to the same pattern from different angles: AI can speed up software work, but speed does not fix unclear goals, weak requirements, missing context, poor priorities, or late feedback. Those are delivery judgment problems.

That is why AI-DIY is built as a lab.

The simulation is built around a small Agile team—not to teach Agile, but because it provides an ideal environment for developing skills you’ll use on virtually any project, whether it’s built by a team or with AI.

The meetings aren’t decoration. Each one gives your team a specific set of tools and documents, so the work can’t run ahead of the thinking. Think of a new feature halfway through a review and you can’t have it — it goes on the list for next time, exactly as it would anywhere real. You never edit the documents yourself either: you tell the team what to change and they change it. The discipline is built into the software, not printed in a handbook.

The lab structure is the thinking structure: clarify the goal, define success, break the work into testable pieces, review what comes back, and decide what happens next.

Skip that sequence and AI may still produce something impressive. But impressive output is not the same as useful software.

The course explains the thinking. The lab makes you practice it. The generated app you guided the team to build shows whether your decisions led to something useful.
